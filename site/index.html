Bukidnon · TB-DOTS referral support

Fewer people lost between the barangay and the TB-DOTS centre.

TB‑Screen supports Barangay Health Workers through the DOH‑NTP symptom checklist, refers presumptive cases to the right TB‑DOTS facility, and keeps track of whether the patient actually went.

Download for Android See how it works

TB‑Screen does not diagnose tuberculosis and produces no score of any kind. Diagnosis happens at a TB‑DOTS facility, through laboratory testing.

What it covers

22 municipalities
The whole of Bukidnon province, with all 464 barangays bundled onto the phone so the address list works with no signal.
11 TB-DOTS centres
Every public TB-DOTS facility in the province, each mapped to the municipalities it serves so the right one is pre-selected.
3 languages
English, Tagalog and Cebuano — every screen, every consent script, and every reminder text.

How it works

Four steps, from a home visit to a recorded result. Each step happens on a different screen, in a different place, often days apart. The point of the system is that the next person always knows what the last one did.

  1. Step 1

    The BHW enrols the patient

    Name, birthdate, sex and barangay, with consent taken first and read aloud in the patient’s own language. No signal needed.

    On the Android app
  2. Step 2

    The checklist decides

    The DOH‑NTP symptom questions, asked one at a time. The answers alone decide whether to refer — nothing is weighted or scored.

    On the Android app
  3. Step 3

    The facility receives it

    The referral reaches the TB‑DOTS centre’s inbox as soon as the phone has signal. Staff collect the sputum sample on site, then record attendance and the laboratory result.

    On the web portal
  4. Step 4

    The check-up is followed up

    Reminders go out three days ahead and the day before. A missed appointment raises a follow-up on the BHW’s home screen.

    Automatic, by SMS

What actually triggers a referral

One rule, written down, the same for every health worker and every barangay. A BHW can explain it to a patient in a sentence, and a reviewer can check it. An answer of “unsure” is never counted as a yes.

Refer if any one of these
  • Cough 2 weeks or more
  • Fever
  • Night sweats
  • Weight loss
  • Coughing blood
OR

The patient has been in close contact with someone known to have TB, together with any one symptom above.

TB-Screen does not diagnose tuberculosis.

This is a limit of the tool by design, not a gap waiting to be filled.

What it does

  • Records the symptoms a patient reports, in their own language.
  • Applies the DOH‑NTP checklist and flags a presumptive case.
  • Refers that patient to a named TB‑DOTS facility and tracks whether they went.
  • Shows the facility’s recorded result back to the health worker afterwards.

What it never does

  • Tell anyone whether a person has TB.
  • Produce a risk score, a probability or a percentage — there is no score anywhere in the system.
  • Use machine learning, cough analysis or any predictive model.
  • Replace a clinician’s judgement or a laboratory test.

Diagnosis of tuberculosis belongs to a TB‑DOTS facility, through laboratory testing. A referral from this app means a person should be tested, and nothing more than that. Every screen the health worker sees says so, and so does the printed referral slip.

What is in it

Built for the conditions the work actually happens in. Patchy signal, shared phones, three languages in one municipality, and a patient who may never come back if nobody follows up.

Offline enrolment and screening

The full workflow runs with no connection. When the phone gets data, records sync one at a time — a single record that fails cannot hold back the others.

Reminders that say nothing clinical

Check-up reminders three days ahead and the day before, plus a nudge if an appointment is missed. The text never mentions TB — anyone holding the phone can read it.

An optional printed referral slip

The referral reaches the facility the moment it syncs. A patient who wants something on paper can carry a printed copy with a QR code — it identifies the referral without printing anything about their condition.

English, Tagalog and Cebuano

Every screen, every consent script and every reminder. A patient hears the consent read in the language they actually speak, and each patient’s reminders go out in theirs.

Barangay-level view for captains

Counts are aggregated per barangay and no finer. There is no household or address-level clustering, and this is not contact tracing.

Access enforced in the database

Every table has row-level security, so a facility account can only read its own patients. The rules are not hidden in the interface where they could be worked around.

Installing it

TB-Screen is not on the Play Store, so Android will ask you to allow the install once. That is normal for programme apps handed out this way.

  1. Tap Download the app

    The file saves to your Downloads folder.

  2. Open the downloaded file

    Tap it from the notification, or find it in Files under Downloads.

  3. Allow the install when asked

    Android shows a warning about apps from this source. Choose Settings, turn on the permission for your browser or Files app, then go back and tap Install.

  4. Sign in and set your own password

    Use the temporary password you were given. The app will ask you to replace it straight away, so nobody else can sign in as you.

About the project

An undergraduate capstone, built against a real problem.

Research title

TB‑Screen BHW: Trilingual Mobile and Web‑Based Referral System Linking Barangay Health Workers and TB‑DOTS Facilities for Tuberculosis Pre‑Screening and Follow‑Up

Back in my first year, I was already determined to align my capstone project with healthcare. However, the exact direction remained unclear until I realized I wanted to focus on something deeply neglected—a condition heavily stigmatized, where those affected are often too afraid to admit their struggle. That realization led me directly to developing the TB‑Screen System.

The system was designed around one constraint that shaped everything else: a barangay health worker is not a clinician, and software should not ask them to act like one. So TB‑Screen records and refers, and leaves every clinical judgement to the TB‑DOTS facility. There is no score to misread and no prediction to over-trust.

How patient information is handled

Consent comes first

The consent script is read to the patient in their own language before anything is recorded, and the toggle is never pre-ticked.

A number only if it is wanted

A mobile number is stored only alongside consent to reminders, enforced by the database itself. Declining never blocks enrolment.

Nothing clinical leaves the system

Reminder texts and printed referral slips carry no condition, no diagnosis and no result — only where to go and when.
